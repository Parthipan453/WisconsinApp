Course Requisites

Requisites represent the academic preparation required to be successful in a course. They are enforced via the enrollment system, meaning a student who attempts to enroll in a course but lacks the requisite preparation will be prevented from enrolling in the course until the requisite has been met.

It is assumed that courses in progress at the time of enrollment will be completed successfully and thus fulfill a course requisite. Course administrators/departments may check the completion of these courses and may drop enrolled students who failed or dropped a requisite course and notify them accordingly.

There are occasions when a student may have the necessary preparation to be successful in a course, but this preparation is not easily identifiable in their student record. An example would be earned transfer credit that did not equate to a UW-Madison course that meets the spirit of the required course. You may ask the course instructor for permission to enroll in a course for which you do not meet the enforced requisite. Instructor permission will override any restrictions on enrollment the class might have.

UW-Madison Courses

Courses identified in requisites list the subject(s) and catalog number (example: MATH 221). If a course is cross-listed, you will meet the requisite regardless of the subject you enrolled, with one exception: minimum credits in a subject. For example, ZOOLOGY/BIOLOGY 101 is the same course, so requisites recognize both Zoology and Biology as fulfilling the requirement; it does not matter which subject you enrolled in.

Concurrent EnrollmentConcurrent enrollment means being enrolled in a course in the same term as the requisite course. There are three ways these can be written:
  1. You may either have completed the requisite course in a prior term or be currently enrolled in it. Example: PLANTSCI 335.
  2. You must be enrolled in listed courses during the same term, every time the courses are offered. You will have to enroll in all courses at the same time otherwise you will not meet the requisite. Examples: NURSING 726 and NURSING 728.
  3. One course requires concurrent enrollment but the other course does not. Enroll in Course B first, then go back and enroll in Course A. Examples: MUS PERF 201 and MUS PERF 251.
Minimum Credits in a SubjectRequisites may specify a certain number of credits in any given subject (example: 3 credits in HISTORY). This means you must have taken at least 3 credits in the HISTORY course array without specifying an individual course. If you take a course cross-listed with HISTORY, but do not enroll in HISTORY, you will not meet the requisite. Example: HISTORY 200.
GradesIn very limited situations, a minimum grade may be included associated with a requisite course (example: a grade of C in Course A). If you are a transfer student, you may need individual permission from the instructor to enroll in courses with this type of requisite. Example: NUTR SCI 431.

Testing Scores

AP, IB, and CLEP

Qualifying Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate, and CLEP scores can be converted to UW–Madison credit. When an exact course match is not available, credit may appear as a transfer equivalent on the academic record rather than as a numbered UW course.

Placement Exams

University and departmental placement exams can demonstrate preparation in English, ESL, French, German, mathematics, Spanish, and other subjects. A requisite may use wording such as placement into a named course.

Transfer Equivalencies

Previous coursework is evaluated and converted to UW–Madison course credit when possible. Coursework without an exact course match may be posted as a subject transfer equivalent and may still meet relevant requisites.

Designations and Transfer CreditSome transfer equivalents represent course content but do not carry every UW–Madison course designation. The enrollment system may account for these equivalents when evaluating a requisite.
Grades for Transfer EquivalenciesWhen a requisite includes a minimum letter grade, a transfer equivalent is evaluated using the standard grade value established for that equivalent in the requisite check.
Standard Equivalencies Without DesignationsSome standard transfer equivalents are used in requisites for course content that normally includes a designation, such as bioscience content or Communication B content, even when the incoming credit does not carry that designation.

Course Designations

Some requisites can be fulfilled through approved course designations rather than a specific course. This lets a student meet the preparation requirement through different courses with the same approved learning designation.

Approved DesignationsCommunication A, Quantitative Reasoning A, and Quantitative Reasoning B may be used in applicable requisites.

Limitations on Enrollment/Audience

Some courses are designed for a particular audience. Their requisites limit enrollment to students who meet the stated academic or program conditions.

Significant Content OverlapStudents may be prevented from enrolling in a course when they already hold credit for substantially similar material. This prevents duplicate credit for courses with overlapping content.
HonorsSome courses limit enrollment to students declared in an appropriate honors program, a specific school or college honors program, or a particular honors-in-the-major program.
Program LimitationsCourses may require a student to be declared in a named degree, major, program, graduate program, certificate, or pre-program before enrolling.

Class Standing

Standing is based on earned credits, credits in progress during enrollment, and academic career. A standing requirement may include students at that level and above, unless it states that the level is required exclusively. Academic careers include undergraduate, graduate, pharmacy, medical, veterinary, law, and special student careers.

Credits and Standing0–23 credits: Freshman; 24–53: Sophomore; 54–85: Junior; 86 or more: Senior.
Course Number LimitsCourses numbered 100–299 are generally undergraduate courses; 300–699 are open to undergraduate and graduate programs; courses numbered 700 and above are for graduate or professional students.

Consent of Instructor

Some courses require an instructor’s consent. This is common for independent study, directed study, auditions, a first-time permission requirement, or other situations where enrollment needs individual review. Students who have preparation not visible in their record—such as transfer work that does not directly map to a UW–Madison course—may request permission to enroll. Instructor permission can override enrollment restrictions.
