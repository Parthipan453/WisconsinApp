Describe Your Need

Characteristics

These are some common characteristics that funders of students, faculty, and researchers want to understand.

Characteristics may include but are not limited to:

  • Level of study completed
  • Demographic characteristics (e.g. gender, race/ethnicity, single-parent, returning student, etc.)
  • Citizenship/residency (some funders may only be allowed to give to U.S. citizens)
  • Long-term goals
  • Geographic Location of the activity to be funded

Funding Types are another important characteristic, describing how grant money may be spent. Here are some types you're likely to encounter.

  • Study (scholarship, fellowship, doctoral completion, doctoral planning, stipend, etc.)
  • Research
  • Travel
  • Conference attendance
Worksheets

These will help you inventory and frame your characteristics. What you document initially is likely to change or become more complete as search results teach you what is available. Don’t expect your initial self-evaluation to be perfect! Allow it to grow and change as your search develops.

Personal Profile - useful for all grant seekers. It identifies elements of your identity, which qualify you for different opportunities. These will be directly relevant to places you searches and to the terms you search with.

Plan of Study - useful to students seeking educational funding, scholarships, study abroad support, conference or travel support, and similar educational experiences.

Plan of Research - useful to researchers and academics seeking funding for their professional work.
